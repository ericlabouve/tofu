# Inspiration review

All **nine** files in `inspiration/` were opened individually and visually inspected for this round. The directory is named `inspiration`, singular.

| Image | Observed design idea | Application to this cutter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [birds-2.jpg](../inspiration/birds-2.jpg) | Red and white sculpted bird profiles share long curved neck and wing boundaries. | A complementary animal can occupy a tall pocket; its neck must remain substantial enough to cut and release. |
| [birds.jpg](../inspiration/birds.jpg) | Alternating bird silhouettes exchange bulges and concavities along their wings and tails. | Design both sides of a shared line together. Avoid relying on the illustrated feather lines for recognition. |
| [cats-and-birds.png](../inspiration/cats-and-birds.png) | Large cats and pale bird shapes use each other's head, leg, and tail spaces. | The most direct example of giving negative space a second identity. A dog-and-bird direction would require intentional changes to both silhouettes. |
| [cats-and-dogs-2.png](../inspiration/cats-and-dogs-2.png) | Seated cats alternate with tall dog figures; much species recognition comes from internal face details. | Mixed silhouettes need not have equal proportions, but evaluate them without eyes and drawn ears. Front-facing dogs are outside the current first-round brief. |
| [cats-and-dogs.jpg](../inspiration/cats-and-dogs.jpg) | Slender cats and dogs interlock through long bodies, heads, and feet. | Allow unequal body and leg proportions. Do not copy the extremely thin legs into tofu cutting geometry. |
| [elephants.png](../inspiration/elephants.png) | Rounded elephant masses pack together through trunks, tails, and sloping backs. | Broader masses can absorb space. Intricate spirals illustrate why decorative tessellation is not automatically suitable as a cutter. |
| [fish-and-birds.jpg](../inspiration/fish-and-birds.jpg) | Wide fish alternate with contrasting upright bird shapes. | Compact secondary shapes can have a different aspect ratio from the main animal; fish are candidates for wider pockets. |
| [fish.png](../inspiration/fish.png) | Fish rotate around shared junctions, with tails fitting between neighboring bodies. | Rotating a companion can use a pocket more effectively than repeating one orientation. A forked tail can identify a fish without an eye. |
| [kangaroos.png](../inspiration/kangaroos.png) | Different orientations and curved bodies interlock legs and tails across diagonal boundaries. | Vary poses and proportions rather than requiring identical dogs at the rectangle's edges. |

## Direction taken

Keep the owner's preferred A side-profile dog as the starting point. Compare six and nine dogs at the same sheet dimensions. Widen selected perimeter contours while retaining the shared internal lines, creating a family of related profiles instead of nine identical copies.

Explore dog bones as a coherent companion theme and fish as a contrasting, easily recognized outline. Birds are a promising future shared-boundary redesign, but this round does not label the existing leftover blobs as birds. Candidate companions must be explicitly drawn and their surrounding remainder still counted.
